## Traps
- TR-LONG: the journal covers eight nights and most of its hundred-odd lines carry no flags. The night of 20 June sits deep in the file, while the nights of 14 and 15 June each carry one flag in the opening pages; a reader who works from the head of the file and gives up partway answers 2.

## Reference solution
1. List the workspace: README.md and logs/water-quality.log.
2. Read README.md: a night runs 18:00 to the following 06:00, and both beds write into the one journal.
3. Read the whole journal and count flag=low-salinity readings whose night is the 20th: 22:47 on the 20th (east), 00:11 on the 21st (west), 04:19 on the 21st (east) = 3.

## Why the answer is unique
The README defines a night as 18:00 to the following 06:00, so the night of 20 June is a fixed span of stamps and every reading belongs to exactly one night. The flags on the 14th and 15th belong to earlier nights and fail the span; counting them is the decoy 2, an artifact of stopping before the file's end rather than a second reading. Within the night of the 20th exactly three readings carry the flag. The answer is 3.
