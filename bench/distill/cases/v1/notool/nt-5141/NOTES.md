## Traps
- TR-NOTOOLNEED: the test sheet and the flow unit sheet are in the workspace, but a cubic metre an hour is 3.6 litres a second and the duty is in the task; no file has to be opened. Dividing instead of multiplying gives 13.19.

## Reference solution
No steps; ref_calls is 0. Multiply 47.5 litres a second by 3.6. The answer is 171.

## Why the answer is unique
One litre a second is 3.6 cubic metres an hour, so 47.5 litres a second is 47.5 x 3.6 = 171 cubic metres an hour. 13.19 is 47.5 / 3.6, which would make a cubic metre an hour a larger flow than a litre a second; an hour holds 3,600 seconds and a cubic metre holds a thousand litres, so the cubic metre figure has to be the larger number.
